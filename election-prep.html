Before The Ballot

How your ballot's levels of government actually work.

City & County

A county commissioner decides road maintenance, jail funding, and the county property tax rate. A city council member decides zoning, local ordinances, and the police and fire budget — closer to daily life than almost anything else on the ballot.

School District

A school board trustee approves the budget and bond measures, hires the superintendent, and sets attendance boundaries — not curriculum. Curriculum standards are set at the state level, by the State Board of Education.

State

A state legislator decides the state budget, school funding formulas, and most criminal law — the layer of government furthest from daily attention, closest to real power.

(The State Board of Education — the body that actually sets curriculum and adopts textbooks — is its own elected office most voter guides skip entirely.)

Adults under 25 are 20–30 percentage points less knowledgeable than older adults on basic questions — like which level of government handles what — a gap that explains roughly half of the turnout gap between young and older voters.
