Why we're building this

01

The knowledge gap

Fewer than 1 in 5 Americans under 45 could pass the U.S. citizenship test, and Texas ranked among the lowest-scoring states. Among 18–24-year-olds, only 4% could answer four basic civics questions. Sources

02

Big expectations, divided power

A new governor and a divided Legislature. Voters expect sweeping change, but every bill has to clear both chambers.

03

Conditional voters

Young Texans vote when they feel it matters, and sit out when change seems slow.

04

Frustrated with the pace

Government moves slowly, and it's most frustrating for people who were never taught why.

05

Schools can't close it

Civics in schools has been limited and politicized, and graduates won't be reached by any classroom fix.

For Funders

<20%

In a typical 140-day Texas session, fewer than one in five filed bills becomes law. That's how the system is built. The league lets players watch it happen to "their" legislators week by week, so slow progress makes sense instead of feeling like betrayal.

Source: Texas Policy Research, 90th Legislative Session calendar

Meets people where they are

Plenty of young Texans already follow a fantasy league. We use that habit to teach how a bill becomes law, reaching people long past graduation.

Nonpartisan by design

Scoring follows the process, not the politics. Players can draft any legislator from either party and win.

Builds real relationships

Players only email their own legislators, through the app. Freshmen earn 1.5x points, so players follow the new members who are still building their networks and want to hear from constituents.

Built to last

Built by the developer of Govvy, a civic transparency app. Players can dig deeper on Govvy with a free trial for the length of the session.
