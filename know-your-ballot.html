The big idea: most people assume Texas government works like a pyramid with the Governor on top. It doesn't. Texas has a "plural executive" — most statewide offices are elected completely independently of the Governor and of each other. No one here is anyone else's boss.

📍
Proximity usually beats title

The offices that sound biggest — U.S. Senator, President — usually have the least direct say over your daily life. A state legislator or county commissioner decides things you feel every week: your school's funding, your road repairs, your property tax rate. And power doesn't always follow the title you'd expect, even close to home: in most Texas cities, the elected mayor actually has less day-to-day authority than the city manager — a professional administrator hired, and fireable, by the city council, not elected by voters at all.
