TVP's Own Work

Our research: where it started, and what the pilot found

In early 2025, TVP piloted a digital, nonpartisan voter guide for San Antonio's May 3 mayoral election — paired with a post-election survey run in partnership with UTSA — the first real-world test of the idea that became TVP's Information Infrastructure.

Worth noting: this pilot tested one digital guide, in one race, in one city. It's the seed TVP's current two-infrastructure model grew from — not a description of that model today.

2018 · Honors Thesis, University of Oregon

Where this started: an honors thesis

Before starting TVP, Sophia had a different question to answer. In 2018, she wrote her University of Oregon honors thesis, The Institutional Disaffiliates: Understanding Why Millennials Did Not Vote in 2016. It found that registration and accessibility weren't what kept millennials from voting in 2016 — motivation was. Millennials weren't disengaged; they were distrusting and ambivalent about formal politics.

That distinction — not apathy, but distrust — is the throughline from that research to TVP's Information and Social Infrastructure today.

May 2025 · Field Note

The case for San Antonio

Nearly 70% of voters surveyed had never seen a nonpartisan voter guide before. San Antonio was gaining roughly 24,000 new residents a year — mostly people in their early 30s without established local voting habits — while turnout in the May 2025 municipal election fell to a modern low: 5.8% in early voting, under 10% overall. Fast growth plus a real information gap made it a natural test case.

Beyond Our Own Data

Research we build on

TVP's approach also draws on published field experiments and academic research on turnout, civic knowledge, and social capital — not just our own numbers.

Field Experiments

Alliance for Youth Action · 2018 · TX-32

A mail-only nonpartisan voter guide to young voters found no significant turnout effect — a single high-salience-race touch, mail alone.

BallotReady · 2019 · Virginia

A nonpartisan guide delivered via SMS/digital ads to low-propensity young voters moved turnout +0.3 points, significant — SMS slightly outperformed Facebook.

VPC Permission Structure · 2023–24 · KY/PA/VA/MS

Mail reassuring overwhelmed voters they "know enough to vote" was competitive with standard social-pressure GOTV mail among under-35 voters specifically.

Roadmaps to Representation · 2018 · San Francisco

Voter guides and spatial-map tools increased voting closer to one's own views, with effects concentrated among low-knowledge voters.

Civic Knowledge & Social Capital

Stockemer & Rocher · 2016

Adults under 25 are 20–30 points less knowledgeable than older adults on which level of government does what — a gap that explains roughly half the youth turnout gap. (Also cited on Election Prep.)

Robert Putnam

Foundational research on America's declining civic and social capital — the grounding for TVP's Social Infrastructure work and its focus on third places.

Practitioner Guidance

Analyst Institute · GOTV Recommendations, 2022

A practitioner playbook on social pressure, timing, and mode-by-mode tactics (mail, digital, phone, SMS, canvass) synthesized across many campaigns.

Analyst Institute · Annual Civic Engagement Report, 2021

A survey of remote and digital GOTV tactics (SMS, digital ads, postcards, vote-by-mail programs) that emerged from the 2020 cycle.
